## Age model and language evidence

The bounded generator supports the existing **1–99** range. Ages **10–19** and exact tens use the ordinary number followed by **metų**. Other ages use the years-number form **vieni, dveji, treji, ketveri, penkeri, šešeri, septyneri, aštuoneri, devyneri**, preceded by the tens where needed, followed by **metai**. These forms agree with years and are the same for male and female profiles. The generator chooses the attested **vieni** model; it does not claim to enumerate every valid native alternative.

Primary evidence: Rima Jezukevičienė, *Lithuanian Grammar: Paradigms*, Lithuanian University of Health Sciences, [university-hosted grammar PDF](https://portalcris.lsmuni.lt/server/api/core/bitstreams/3fc7cc3b-76bd-49a2-85f0-511838d6e210/content), pp. 14 (number system and agreement), 21 (age construction), 25 (agreement examples with years). No general grammar engine was introduced.

The learner note contrasts 20 with 25, explains when to use metų/metai in plain English, includes the corrected 28 model and a personalised “Your age” anchor. The existing personalised Learn/audio card appears before the speaking task. The Learn card and all production/dialogue targets share the runtime-generated phrase.

DOB is interpreted as a calendar date in the learner's current local timezone, not parsed as UTC midnight. Birthdays increment on the local birthday; February 29 increments on March 1 in non-leap years, preserving the prior intended comparison. Impossible dates are rejected instead of silently rolling over. Missing, invalid, future, age-0 and age-100+ inputs retain the existing age-30 fallback. No profile/account values are rewritten. Age refresh follows the existing profile rebuild lifecycle; no midnight scheduler was added.

Numeric STT equivalence recognises these age forms and their full compound values. A recognised different number, or an extra number, fails before spelling tolerance can accept it. Existing number/cardinal/time tests remain green, including grammatical distinctions when the recogniser returns words. This is an automated matcher check, not a claim to have tested live microphone recognition or a synthetic voice.
